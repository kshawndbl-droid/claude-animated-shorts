# Operation Mincemeat facts (checked 2026-11-24)
Sources: IWM "The war on paper: Operation Mincemeat"; National WWII Museum "Secret Agents, Secret Armies: Operation Mincemeat" (2022).
- Body: Glyndwr Michael, homeless Welshman, died after swallowing rat poison.
- Fake identity: Major William Martin, Royal Marines.
- Planners: Ewen Montagu and Charles Cholmondeley (XX Committee); idea echoed in Ian Fleming's Trout Memo.
- Fake letter (Nye to Alexander): invasion aimed at Greece (and Sardinia implied), Sicily only a cover.
- Pocket litter: fiancee's photo and letters, engagement-ring receipt, theatre ticket stubs, tailor's bills, letter from father.
- Submarine HMS Seraph; body put in the sea early 30 April 1943 off Huelva, Spain; found by a fisherman; crew told canister held weather instruments.
- Spanish opened/photographed the papers; copies reached German intelligence; briefcase returned to British consul 11 May; letters had been removed and resealed.
- Hitler (14 May 1943) sent 1st Panzer Division from France to Greece plus more divisions to the Balkans; Sardinia reinforced.
- Allied invasion of Sicily: 9-10 July 1943; Hitler still expected Greece.
- IWM: "Mincemeat" had been well and truly swallowed.
- Buried in Huelva as William Martin; Glyndwr Michael's name added to the grave in the late 1990s (NWWII: 1997).
